Ex-9-5: Spatial Joins, with an AI Assistant

Abstract
Spatial Data

This is an in-class exercise. Before class, work through the lecture 09-5: Spatial Joins: Matching Rows by Location. In class, you work through the worksheet with an AI assistant writing the code. Your job is everything the AI cannot do: deciding what one row of the result is, knowing how many matches to expect, asking for the right checks, and judging whether the result is right.

Setup

  1. Download the worksheet, ex-9-5.qmd, and the data files:
    • 9-5-corn-yield-points.gpkg and 9-5-treatment-blocks.gpkg
    • 9-5-soy-yield-points.gpkg and 9-5-seed-rate-points.gpkg
    • 9-5-README.txt
  2. Use the R Project folder ch9-practice from the earlier Chapter 9 exercises, or make one, with two subfolders, data and analysis. You may ask the AI how to do this.
  3. Copy the five data files into ch9-practice/data, and the worksheet into ch9-practice/analysis.
  4. Ask the AI to install the sf package if you have not already.
  5. Open the worksheet and start at Task 1.
It worked if

Typing here::here() in the Console prints the path to ch9-practice and nothing longer.

If here::here() prints a path ending in analysis, the .Rproj file was not recognised. here only accepts a .Rproj file whose first line starts with Version:, which every project created in RStudio (File, New Project) has.

Answers

Open each one only after you have tried the task.

Task 1: Look at the corn field

What you should find

A. 3,830 yield points and 25 plots, both in WGS 84 / UTM zone 15N (same CRS: TRUE).

B. No. The plots cover only part of the field; the map shows many yield points between and around them. (The share is the answer to Task 2.)

C. No. The plots do not overlap, so a point can lie in at most one. A point exactly on a shared boundary would match both, which is why the check for “more than one plot” is still worth asking for.

Task 2: Give each yield point its plot

What you should find

3,830 rows, one per yield point, as expected. 2,961 points have no plot; only 869 (23%) lie inside one. 0 points match more than one plot.

This is the main finding of the task. The plots cover 13,917 m² of a field of about 60,420 m², so most of the combine’s points are outside the trial: in the gaps between plots and around them. The files record no nitrogen rate for those points, so they have no target_rate and should not be in an analysis of the trial.

The join kept them, with NA, because a spatial join keeps every target row. Nothing went wrong in the code; the check for points with no plot is what tells you how much of your data the analysis is really about.

Task 3: Average yield by nitrogen rate

What you should find

5 rows, one per rate (105, 135, 165, 195, 215 lb per acre), each applied to 5 plots. The point counts add up to 869:

target_rate n_points mean_yield
105 171 151.1
135 175 138.1
165 175 143.1
195 175 150.8
215 173 138.9

Had the points with no plot stayed in, group_by(target_rate) would have added a sixth row, NA, holding 2,961 points with a mean of 145.2, the largest group in the table. A check of the number of rows (expected 5) catches it.

Task 4 (if time allows): Where is the soybean field?

What you should find

Both files record WGS 84 / UTM zone 28N, in metres (same CRS: TRUE). Transformed to longitude and latitude, the field’s centre is at longitude -16.7, latitude 39.1: in the Atlantic Ocean, west of Portugal. UTM zone 28N covers longitudes 18 to 12 degrees west, nowhere near the US.

So the CRS recorded in the files is wrong. The numbers are metres, as the README says, but they do not belong to zone 28N, and the files do not say which CRS they do belong to. Whoever produced the files has to be asked.

What you can still do:

  1. Yes. Matching within 10 m uses distances computed from the numbers themselves, in two layers that carry the same label. The numbers are metres (README), so distances inside the field are right even though the label is wrong.
  2. No. Joining to counties needs the field’s true location. With the recorded CRS, it matches no county.
  3. No, for the same reason: PRISM covers the US, and the field, as recorded, is in the ocean. The extraction would return NA for every point.

This is why the four checks of lecture 09-1 include the bounding box and a map: a CRS label that is wrong gives no error until you combine the layer with one whose location is right.

Task 5: Match each soybean yield point to nearby seed-rate points

What you should find

The CRS is in metres, so the distance is 10.

1,683 yield points before; 2,076 rows after the join, because 355 yield points have more than one seed-rate point within 10 m (up to 4), and each match is a row. After the summary, 1,683 rows again.

341 yield points have no seed-rate point within 10 m, so their mean seed rate is NA. They should be left out of any analysis of seed rate and yield, and the number left out reported. A larger distance would match more of them, but with seed-rate points farther away that may not describe what was planted at that spot; the distance is a decision about the planter and the combine, not about the code.

Task 6: Maria’s code

Answers

A. The join keeps one row per plot and yield point inside it (869 rows here), plus one row with NA for each plot with no point. The summary computes each row’s plot area, drops the shapes, and adds up the areas and counts the rows for each rate.

B. Five plots of roughly 557 m² each, so about 2,786 m² per rate. Maria gets about 35 times that, because each plot’s area is added once per yield point in it:

target_rate true_area_m2 maria_area_m2 n_points
105 2786 95278 171
135 2778 97237 175
165 2788 97592 175
195 2781 97350 175
215 2783 96288 173

No error: every number is a real area, just added too many times. It is the same failure as Nebraska’s area counted once per well in the lecture.

C. Here, yes: every plot has yield points (0 plots without one). For a plot with no point, n() would count its one NA row as 1 point.

D. A total that must not change: the plots’ area by rate, computed before the join, should equal the one after. Or: sum the areas from the plots layer, not from the joined table, and use the join only for the counts.

E. The AI usually spots the repeated area once it is asked what one row of plot_yield is. Compare its list with yours.
